## Step 1: the static second-order step in the precisions in use

luna-cpp's `order 1` is MUSCL-Hancock (second order), and L1 already differentiated it, but only for `Precision<>`. L2 makes the
step derivatives work for every policy the code runs: double, `grad32`, and `grad16` (the 3D default). The forward is the production
step itself, with its own kernels and its rows as stored. The derivative is that of the double scheme at those rows, computed in
double (owner, 2026-11-19).

**How.**
- `step_tangent` runs step()'s own Sweep (through `time_step_tangent`, below) and FaceFlux for the forward.
- The derivative kernels recompute each face state in double from the stored rows (`linear_side`). The tangent and the adjoint then
  linearise at the same point, whatever the kernels' multiply-add contraction.
- The tangent, flux and update kernels are named functors (TangentPrimitives, TangentSweep, TangentFluxes, TangentUpdate,
  AdjointFaces, AdjointCell, AdjointGather), so ncu can tell them apart.

**Verification** [M] (A100, job 32644995, commit 3122098; host: job 32586043 for L1's, the L2 host run in "Default path"):

| step case (1000 jittered cells) | forward Q against step() | J dq against FD | dot test | against the double policy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| double HLLC, 1 and 3 steps | same bits | 4.6e-10, 3.6e-10 | 3e-17, 2e-16 | — |
| double HLL, 1 step | same bits | 5.1e-10 | 1.6e-16 | — |

## Speed of the derivatives

A100, 128³ static jittered (2.10M cells, 16.7M faces), HLLC, the derivative test's `--cost` (median of 5). The forward is
`time_step(0.3) + step(dt)`; the tangent is `time_step_tangent(0.3, dq) + step_tangent(dt, dq)`.

| | L1 (2026-11-19) | now, double | now, grad16 |
|---|---|---|---|
| forward step | 13.0 ms | 13.0 ms | 10.7 ms |
| forward + 1 tangent | 40.1 ms (3.07x; L1's 2.32x had no time_step in it) | 24.2 ms (1.86x) | 26.0 ms (2.43x) |
| adjoint step | 50.3 ms (3.85x) | 32.0 ms (2.46x) | 31.2 ms (2.91x) |

Targets (owner): tangent at or below 1.5x, adjoint at or below 3x. The adjoint is there on both policies. The tangent is at 1.86x
(double) and 2.43x (grad16).

**Levers**, each measured against the one before and with a docs/tried.md row:

| lever | effect at 128³ double | kept |
|---|---|---|
| HLLC upwind state copied entry by entry in tangent and adjoint | tangent fluxes 9.28 -> 7.93 ms, adjoint fluxes 21.8 -> 19.1 (spills of 2.4 and 10 kB/cell gone) | yes |
| two list walks in the gradient derivatives | tangent sweep 24.8 -> 18.4 ms, adjoint sweep 14.2 -> 10.5 | yes |
| flux adjoint once per face (80 B a face of bars, gathered per cell in list order) | adjoint 44.0 -> 36.2 ms | yes |
| F and dF in one 80-B row | tangent update 5.47 -> 3.68 ms | yes |
| side adjoint folded into the gradient adjoint's walk | time equal, -120 B/cell | yes (memory) |
| one Riemann solve for the tangent's flux and value | tangent fluxes 8.07 -> 7.70 ms | yes |
| the tangent of the gradients inside the forward Sweep (a hook; `time_step_tangent`) | tangent 30.2 -> 27.1 ms | yes |
| the adjoint's fit stored by its forward recompute (440 B/cell) | adjoint 36.3 -> 32.5 ms | yes |
| the sweep hook takes the forward's clips (no gradient copy, the clip's face instead of its offset) | tangent 27.1 -> 24.2 ms | yes |
| the adjoint's gather from face rows | adjoint 32.0 -> 32.5 ms | no |

**Decision (the owner, 2026-11-22): the reverse step keeps the faster version.** It keeps the stored fit (+440 B/cell) and the face bars
(+640 B/cell, 80 B a face). The gather from face rows, which would save 220 B/cell for 1.5 % more time, is not taken.

**Where the tangent's time goes now** (128³ double, ms):
- fused sweep and dt: 12.3, against the forward's 7.5;
- fluxes: 7.7, against 3.4;
- update: 3.7, against 1.9;
- primitives: 0.9, against 0.3.

ncu on the previous state (job 32610565) put the fused sweep at the 255-register limit with spills, the flux kernel at 12.5 % of warps
active with 158 registers and no spills, and the update at 84 % of DRAM peak.

**What 1.5x would take** [I]: the tangent's extra work must fit in 6.5 ms, about the forward's sweep. Three levers are left:
- the fused sweep's own registers (its double fit duplicates the forward's on a double policy only partly);
- the flux kernel's occupancy;
- k tangents per pass, which amortises every gather over k but does not lower the cost of one tangent.

On grad16 the fused sweep keeps a double fit of its own beside the forward's float one, which costs the 2.43x.

**The exactly flat Sod with MC is a convention, not an error.** The Sod state is exactly flat on both sides of the jump, where MC has
no derivative.
- **LUNA's jvp takes the zero branch of `where(a b > 0, …, 0)` there:** the first-order tangent that LUNA's AUTODIFF.md names as
  its flat-state weakness.
- **Ours takes the unlimited central slope**, which is what LUNA's own fix (`limiter_tangent_eps`, minmod only) gives at a = b = 0.
- **The evidence:** the same problem with every cell perturbed (no cell exactly flat) agrees with LUNA, and so do minmod (whose
  conventions coincide: both 0 where sL sR <= 0) and the sound wave. The first perturbed attempt still differed: it scaled U, and
  Sod's zero momentum stayed exactly flat.

## Step 4: the moving step at fixed topology (first part: 2D, order 0)

On a moving mesh the state is (Q, x). The geometry depends on the positions, and the positions move with the point velocities,
which depend on W. What is in place:
- **`MovingFace<Riemann, D>`** (euler.hpp): the face flux |A| F(L, R, A/|A|, w) and its tangent and adjoint in all its inputs
  (L, R, the area vector, the face velocity). It reuses `flux_tangent` and `flux_adjoint` (dL, dR at fixed n, w) through the
  solver's two invariances:
  - Galilean: a change dw of w moves both sides' velocities by −dw and adds to_lab's own derivative;
  - rotation: dn ⟂ n is the rotation Ω = dn nᵀ − n dnᵀ, so dF = Ω F + J(−Ω L, −Ω R, −Ω w).
- **`GeometryDerivative<D, Memory>`** (geometry_derivative.hpp): the interface the moving step reads, a tangent dx → (dV, dc, dA,
  dx_f) and its transpose. The mesh library is to provide it (the voronoi-cpp issue below). Until it does, the tests use
  `reference::Geometry2D` (src/test/reference_geometry_2d.hpp, host, test-only), built from the mesh's triangles: circumcentres,
  the face segments, and the cells' pyramid sums as tet_cells.hpp has them.
- **`FiniteVolume::step_moving_tangent(dt, dq, dx, geometry)` and `step_moving_adjoint(dt, qbar, xbar, geometry)`**: the moving
  step at order 0 (advance<0, true>() + move()). The forward is step() itself. The derivative follows the step's dependencies:
  - the primitives through V(x);
  - the point velocity: v plus the regularisation, through the cell centroid, c_s and the largest face angle;
  - AREPO's face velocity, through the separation, x_f and the w's;
  - the face flux;
  - x' = x + dt w.

  The quantisation of x to the mesh grid is taken as the identity. The adjoint gathers per cell and per face, with no atomics.
  The code is correctness first: plain kernels and per-call arrays, so its speed is not yet measured.

**Verification** [M] (A100, job 32723275), all checks pass:

  a 1 % error in one entry is caught.
- **The 2D geometry reference** (32 × 32 jittered periodic mesh, the points on the grid and moved by up to 1e3 whole grid steps,

- **The 2D moving step at order 0** (32 × 32, the state (Q, x)): the forward is step()'s bits.

  | case | J (dq, dx) against FD | dot test |

## voronoi-cpp issue draft (not filed): geometry derivatives at fixed topology

**Title:** Geometry derivatives at fixed topology: cell volumes, centroids, face area vectors and face centroids with respect to the generator positions

**What.** For the mesh as it stands after `update()` and `update_cells()`, provide the derivatives of the geometry with respect to the generator positions x, at the current topology (the same tetrahedra or triangles, faces and cell lists):
- cell volumes V_i and cell centroids c_i (`CellFaces::volume`, `CellFaces::centroid`);
- face area vectors A_f and face centroids x_f (`Geometry::area`, `Geometry::centroid`).

**Who needs it.** Any user who differentiates a computation on a Voronoi mesh with respect to its generators, for example:
- shape and mesh optimisation (centroidal Voronoi tessellations, energy functionals of V and A);
- inverse problems and sensitivity studies on moving-mesh solvers.

The first user is luna-cpp's moving-mesh finite-volume step. Its hand-written tangent and adjoint are done on a static mesh and need these derivatives for a moving one.

**Requested API.**
- **Tangent (forward mode):** given dx (one vector per generator), return dV, dc, dA and dx_f.
- **Adjoint (reverse mode):** given bars of V, c, A and x_f, return x̄ (one vector per generator).
- **Where:** on the device, from the mesh's current arrays.
- **Determinism:** the adjoint gathers per generator over its tetrahedra/triangles or faces in a fixed order, with no atomics, so it gives the same bits every run.
- **Dimensions:** 2D and 3D.

**Where the derivative comes from.**
- Each Voronoi vertex is the circumcentre of a Delaunay tetrahedron (triangle in 2D). That is a rational function of its corners' offsets, with a closed-form 3×9 Jacobian (2×4 in 2D) in the three (two) offsets from corner 0.
- A face is the polygon of circumcentres around a Delaunay edge (in 2D, the segment between two). Its area vector and centroid are sums over that ring.
- The cell volume is Σ_f |A_f · d_f| / (2D), with d_f the separation to the neighbour. The cell centroid is the pyramid-weighted mean, at D/(D+1) of the way to each x_f, as `tet_cells.hpp` already sums them.
- The tangent is the chain rule through these steps; the adjoint is its transpose.

**Quantised positions.** The geometry is computed from the offsets between generators in whole steps of the 2³⁰ grid (`rounded_offsets`, `CircleAs`), so as a function of the double positions it is piecewise constant at the 2⁻³⁰ scale.
- The derivative asked for is that of the same formulas, with the offsets treated as continuous. This is the derivative of the geometry the user would get without quantisation, which is what an optimiser or a tangent model needs.
- Slivers computed exactly on the integer grid are the same rational function, so they take the same derivative.
- Periodic images enter through the nearest-image offset, whose derivative is 1.

**At a flip.** A flip changes which faces exist, and has no derivative.
- The geometry is continuous across a flip: the face that appears or vanishes has zero area at the cospherical (cocircular) configuration.
- So the fixed-topology derivative is a valid one-sided derivative, and an optimiser sees a kink there, not a jump.
- Nothing beyond this is asked about flips: the caller differentiates whatever topology `update()` produced.

**Verification.**
- The tangent against central differences in the positions, on jittered meshes, with h well above the grid step (the quantisation is 2⁻³⁰ of the box) and no flip within ±h.
- The adjoint against the tangent with the dot test, ⟨w, J v⟩ = ⟨Jᵀw, v⟩.
- Identities, to rounding:
  - Σ_i dV_i = 0 in a periodic box;
  - Σ_f dA_f = 0 over each cell's faces, with signs;
  - a uniform translation dx gives dV = dA = 0, and dc = dx_f = 0 for the centroids relative to their generator.
- On the host and on the A100, with the cost reported against one geometry pass (`update_cells`).

**Memory.** Recompute rather than store. The circumcentres are already held, and the Jacobian of one tetrahedron is cheap to rebuild. No per-face rows are needed beyond what the mesh already keeps. Derivatives are in double, also when the float face rows (`FaceRows`, `centroid32`) are allocated.

**Out of scope.** Derivatives through `update()` itself (flip decisions, repair, re-sorts) and through the quantisation.
