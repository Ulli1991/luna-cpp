# Roofline of the hydro, Sedov 3D, one A100, 2026-11-05

Sedov 3D jittered, HLLC, double, order 1, face limiter, Morton order, 40 steps. luna-cpp 28a647d, voronoi-cpp 0b433f0, cuda 13.0. Not repeated
after the pin moved to ffb6060 (mesh fixes): the branch sits on that main, the numbers are from the older pin. Runs, logs:
voronoi-cpp/build-roofline/ (`runs/`, `logs/`, `roofline.json`). Script: `scripts/roofline_a100.sh`; tables `scripts/roofline_table.py`,
`roofline_regime.py`; figures `scripts/roofline_figures.py`; peak kernels `src/bench/roof_peak.cpp`. Each job is one GPU on ravg1002 and has that GPU's
clock, 1095 or 1305 MHz. Compare within a job.

**The limit of the whole step is DRAM bytes per cell, and nothing is at that limit yet.** The step sits at about 1.9 flop/B, left of the ridge at
5.3, at 45 % of the sustained bandwidth and 16 % of the FP64 peak. Only `update` is at the bandwidth roof. The largest kernel, the gradient sweep, is
latency-bound, 3.1-3.6 x from its roof.

## How flops are counted

SASS instruction counts of ncu (2026.2.1): `sm__sass_thread_inst_executed_op_{dfma,dadd,dmul}_pred_on`, flop = 2 DFMA + DADD + DMUL; compares and min/max
are not flops. FMA as 2 checked: `roof::fma` is 137.47 G DFMA for 137.44 G operations. Division and square root are FMA sequences and count as such:
ncu on `roof::div` and `roof::sqrt` gives 7 DFMA + 1 DMUL = 15 flop and 5 DFMA + 3 DMUL = 13 flop. Time, cycles and DRAM bytes: hardware counters,
`--clock-control none`, L2 flushed per kernel (ncu default) except `regime`. The sum of ncu's kernel times matches the job's own phases: 15.24 against
15.37 ms (static), 23.84 against 23.80 (hydro, two passes). Window: two steps of steps 5-8, early in the blast. Flops per face were equal at 128^3 and
256^3; late in the run was not measured.

Analytic count by hand from the source, branch through the HLLC star state (the case in most of the box), with the division and root above:

| | analytic | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| fluxes static, flop/face | 511 | 488 | -4.5 % |
| fluxes moving, one pass | 631 | 608 | -3.6 % |
| fluxes pass 1 (no half step) | 471 | 458 | -2.8 % |
| fluxes pass 2 | 647 | 620 | -4.2 % |
| gradient sweep static, flop/cell | 3370-3466 | 3091 | -8 to -11 % |

Static: 337 + 10 divisions + 3 roots less 15 terms in w = 0 that fold. The sweep: 109 + a root per list entry for the fit, 34-40 for the limiter, 15.9 entries
per cell, 140 + 46 divisions + 4 roots for the Cholesky solve. The measured counts lie a little under: sharing by the compiler, and a limiter that skips the sum
where the gradient is exactly zero. The per-face count is confirmed; the sweep to about 10 %.

Three regimes:
- Below about 64^3 the fixed cost per step rules: launches and fences, 0.03 ms a kernel, not the memory. It takes the mesh longest: 50 launches, 2 ms, 31 % of the step at 64^3. A step
  is 6 % fixed at 128^3 and under 1 % at 256^3.
- The cache-to-DRAM change is at 24^3 to 48^3, the 32-64 MiB L2 of the pointer chase. It is nearly invisible in throughput: net of t0 the hydro does 145-164 M/s in L2 against
  137 in DRAM, +6 to +20 %. The reading: L2 latency is only 1.75 x under DRAM's (191 against 334 ns), and 13 kB/cell go through L2 for 4 from DRAM, so a latency-bound step loses little when it leaves L2.
- From 96^3 up flat, to 256^3: static within 5 %, no fall at 40 GB, none of the TLB kind (chase 334 ns flat to 8 GiB). Mesh still rises (143 -> 221 M/s) because its fixed part
  fades later. 256^3 two passes: 40.5 of 42.4 GB, the end of the memory axis.

LUNA's latency: at 128^3 the fixed 0.77 ms is a quarter of its step, so it is still in the latency regime there (683-691 M/s) and leaves it near 192^3 (9 %). Its curve is also not one curve:
slow rounds of the kind in research_notes/luna_slow_mode: 64^3 126 / 261 / 260, 32^3 15 / 43 / 16, 192^3 825 / 817 / 680; in job 31928673 96^3 294 and 192^3 703. Median of three is plotted.

LUNA's faces kernel is arithmetic-bound, right of the ridge, x 1.25 from its pipe. It has our sweep's registers and warps (152 registers, 18 %), so these alone do not make a kernel slow: with
shared-memory blocks its warps stall 2.5 % of the time against our 64 %. The structured grid costs 132 B/cell at 1939 flop/cell; ours fetches 7 to 11 times the bytes per cell, 4 to 5 times per flop. The
difference is indirect addressing per face, not arithmetic. Most other kernels of LUNA's step ran at 84-90 % of DRAM peak (job 31877863, minmod, 256^3).
